Within 5\,ms the mean numbers of completed proposals per target were 6.94 for drifting, 7.05 for one Euler step, 0.42 for 16 steps, 19.98 for retrieval and zero for uFlowCSP. Drifting led 16-step flow by 42.97 percentage points in recovery (paired 95\% interval $[36.72,49.48]$). Its difference from one Euler step was $-3.13$ points ($[-9.11,3.13]$).

\paragraph{Sensitivity to the definition of a match.}
For the same 128 binary targets and twenty proposals, requiring a maximum-site fit reduced CrytalDrift recovery from 74.74\% to 37.76\%. Corresponding maximum-site rates were 40.10\% for one Euler step, 46.61\% for 16 steps, 57.81\% for retrieval and 82.81\% for uFlowCSP. Keeping the original RMS match but also requiring native-volume agreement on the same proposal gave 41.41\%, 55.21\%, 58.85\%, 39.84\% and 87.50\% in that order. The drift-minus-one-Euler difference under this volume rule was $-13.80$ percentage points (paired 95\% interval $[-21.09,-6.25]$). A rescaled arrangement can match even when the generated cell volume is substantially wrong, one example of the limitations of structural matching as a benchmark \cite{matcherlimitations}. The per-target records retain both tests for every proposal.
