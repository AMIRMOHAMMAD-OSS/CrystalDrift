# TokenDrift for crystal generation

**Research question.** Can a TokenDrift refinement of an existing discrete crystal generator recover the distribution of occupied symmetry sites in fewer denoising steps? The natural first output is a *protostructure*. It specifies the space group, the symmetry-related sets of sites and the elements occupying them. Several cells and sets of continuous coordinates can realize the same protostructure. This distinction determines the choice of model, embedding and evaluation.

## What we checked on real structures

Two MP-20 training records, **mp-1094439** and **mp-1094198**, both have conventional composition Mg₄Zn₈ and space group 63 (*Cmcm*). The first places zinc on two independent 4c orbits. The second places zinc on one 8g orbit. A feature containing only elemental atom counts gives both the same value. Counting symmetry orbits separately for Mg and Zn gives (1, 2) and (1, 1). Changing the cell origin cannot change how many independent orbits an element occupies. The complete 27,136-record training split contains 57 composition-and-group combinations exhibiting this particular distinction. The same orbit counts can also describe distinct structures, so 57 is a lower bound for ambiguities in these data. [Data release](https://github.com/httk/wyckoffdiff/blob/main/data/README.md) · [Reproducible count and examples](orbit-probe.md)

**One update of a working loss.** I restricted a categorical head to these two known assignments and initialized each at probability 0.5. With eight copies of the two-4c structure and two of the one-8g structure as real references, one TokenDrift-style gradient step raised the two-4c probability to **0.5125**. Reversing the mixture lowered it to **0.4875**. A balanced reference kept it at 0.5. Replacing orbit counts with composition kept it at 0.5 in all three cases. These are deliberately chosen mixture weights. This checks that the loss can move valid count logits in the intended direction. The change in probability depends on the step size. It supplies no estimate of crystal stability, occurrence probabilities in nature or gains from training a denoiser.

Run `python drift_step.py` in this directory to repeat the update with the saved features. It uses the current batch as its entire reference set, excludes self-negatives and averages two temperature-normalized drift fields. In the first check all ten generated features start at the same midpoint, so their repulsion term vanishes. A second check initializes five examples at 25% and five at 75%, allowing repulsion between distinct features. The mean output probability changes from 0.5 to 0.4944, 0.5000 or 0.5056 for a 20%, 50% or 80% target. The balanced case stays still by symmetry even though the soft midpoint differs from either hard example. This run uses a two-state categorical head and no feature queue. ## What to read first

| Work and passages | Representation and training setup | What to reuse or test |
|---|---|---|
| CrystaLLM, Antunes et al., Methods §§4.1–4.3 and Supplementary Note 1 | A decoder predicts the next token in a standardized crystallographic information file (CIF). Its 371-symbol vocabulary includes elements, space groups, CIF tags, digits and punctuation. The authors assembled about 2.3 million structures from the Materials Project, OQMD and NOMAD. | A baseline that writes complete structures from text prompts. The curation rule keeps one structure per cell-composition and space-group pair, selecting the smallest volume per formula unit. This removes within-pair alternatives, a decisive limitation for a polymorph-frequency experiment. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04340) · [Authors’ code](https://github.com/lantunes/CrystaLLM) |
| Wyckoff Transformer (WyFormer), Kazeev et al., §§2.1–2.3, Appendix N | One global space group plus an unordered set of orbit tokens. Each orbit has an element, site symmetry and enumeration to distinguish positions with the same site symmetry. Three lookup embeddings are concatenated. The Transformer has no sequence-position encoding. Equivalent crystallographic settings are sampled during training. | A blueprint for a set encoder and a strong autoregressive protostructure baseline. Randomizing equivalent settings during training and omitting sequence positions solve different problems. The model generates templates. The paper uses a separate reconstruction stage to obtain cell and coordinates. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02407v4) · [Code and preprocessing](https://github.com/SymmetryAdvantage/WyckoffTransformer) |
| WyckoffDiff, Ekström Kelvinius et al., §§3.1–3.6, Algorithm 1 and §4 | A discrete denoising diffusion model conditions on space group. Each fixed Wyckoff position has a categorical choice of element or vacancy. Each position with free coordinates has a separate categorical **repeat count** for each element. A graph network predicts distributions for both types of variable. | Closest implementation base. Both heads expose clean-state probabilities that can enter a differentiable loss. Treating all sites as one element-or-vacancy choice would erase repeated orbits. The original training used WBM. The authors also release processed MP-20 and Carbon-24 splits. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06485) · [Code](https://github.com/httk/wyckoffdiff) · [Dataset files](https://github.com/httk/wyckoffdiff/blob/main/data/README.md) |
| TokenDrift, Oba et al., §§3.1–3.5, Algorithm 1 and §4.1 | For a categorical prediction $p$, the soft token is the embedding average $pE$. A frozen encoder produces a feature. Real examples attract completed features and other model examples repel them. A detached feature target provides a gradient back to the logits. The authors refine pretrained text denoisers and evaluate the original sampler at fixed numbers of evaluations. | Transfer the loss interface, the exclusion of self from negative references and the matched continuation control. Its text experiments establish a few-step refinement result. For a model specifically designed for single-pass text synthesis, see Seq-Drifting. [TokenDrift](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19470v1) · [Authors’ project page](https://daioba.github.io/tokendrift/) · [Seq-Drifting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12879) |
| Wren and Wrenformer, Goodall et al. and Riebesell et al. | Wren embeds the chemical elements and symmetry orbits without requiring coordinates. WyckoffDiff uses features from a pretrained Wrenformer for its Fréchet Wrenformer Distance. | A candidate frozen crystal feature space. The usual encoder reads hard labels. Feeding mixtures into its embedding layer, dealing with zero occupancy and checking that the adapted encoder reproduces hard inputs are implementation work. A property-prediction embedding can merge distinct structures. [Wren](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11132) · [Wrenformer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-01055-1) · [WyckoffDiff, §3.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06485#S3.SS6) |

## Which dataset answers which question?

| Dataset | Suitable use and limitations |
|---|---|
| MP-20 | Start with the released train/validation/test material IDs for this 45,231-structure Materials Project benchmark, with at most 20 atoms in the primitive cell. The WyckoffDiff release supplies processed Wyckoff labels alongside CIFs. A space-group-conditioned run can compare the distribution of hard assignments within groups with enough independent support. Report those group sizes. [DiffCSP data](https://github.com/jiaor17/DiffCSP/tree/main/data) · [Wyckoff labels and splits](https://github.com/httk/wyckoffdiff/blob/main/data/README.md) |
| WBM | The closest reproduction of the WyckoffDiff study. These structures come from chemical substitutions into known prototypes and subsequent calculations. This construction makes prototype frequency and overlap important. Its released splits offer held-out evaluation in addition to the authors’ training-set comparisons. [Original dataset paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-020-00481-6) · [Processed splits](https://github.com/httk/wyckoffdiff/blob/main/data/README.md) |
| Carbon-24 and Perov-5 | Carbon-24 removes variation in element identity and stresses structure. Perov-5 is a narrow five-atom perovskite benchmark that can make a constrained workflow easier to debug. Neither distribution estimates equilibrium polymorph populations. [Carbon-24 source](https://doi.org/10.24435/materialscloud:2020.0026/v1) · [Benchmark releases](https://github.com/jiaor17/DiffCSP/tree/main/data) |
| MPTS-52 | A chronological transfer test with up to 52 atoms in the primitive cell. DiffCSP reports 27,380 training, 5,000 validation and 8,096 test entries. Use the published IDs and check overlap if pretraining on other crystal corpora. [DiffCSP, §5.1](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/38b787fc530d0b31825827e2cc306656-Paper-Conference.pdf) |
| Alex-MP-20 and the CrystaLLM corpus | MatterGen's larger corpus contains roughly 600,000 filtered Materials Project and Alexandria structures. Its data release and evaluation code are valuable when moving to relaxed stability, uniqueness and novelty. CrystaLLM provides a large serialized-text corpus. The sampling and curation rules differ substantially across these releases. [MatterGen data](https://github.com/microsoft/mattergen/blob/main/data-release/alex-mp/README.md) · [MatterGen evaluation](https://github.com/microsoft/mattergen) · [CrystaLLM Methods](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04340) |

Database occurrences measure a database sampling process. For a frequency experiment with a known answer, construct an explicitly weighted mixture. For formula-only structure prediction, withhold the target space group and Wyckoff pattern. A second protocol may supply the group as a known constraint. A retrieval baseline and a split withholding entire prototype families make it easier to interpret a match on a new material ID. [Prototype-controlled benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26502) · [Simple crystal discovery baselines](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/mh/d5mh00010f)

## Smallest informative adaptation

1. **Choose the target.** Model the empirical distribution of decorated symmetry-site assignments conditional on a known space group. Use the same conditioning, number of candidates and checkpoint for every training arm. An exact-composition condition requires a separate multiplicity-aware constraint or sampler.
2. **Build the feature input.** On a fixed site, replace the hard element lookup with the probability-weighted element/vacancy embedding. On a repeatable site, use the probability-weighted count embedding for each element. Include site symmetry and multiplicity. Use a set or graph encoder with no dependence on row order, and either standardize equivalent settings consistently or average over their induced relabelings. Freeze the encoder parameters while allowing gradients through its input. Encode true templates on the same path using one-hot categories.
3. **Check the embedding before training.** A chemistry-only sum assigns equal values to two structures with the same composition. Verify that physically equivalent re-encodings agree, that hard one-hot inputs reproduce the reference encoding and that demonstrably distinct decorations remain distinguishable. Matching features of uncertain soft tokens need not give calibrated frequencies after hard sampling.
4. **Isolate the effect.** Continue the same denoiser with its original objective, with the TokenDrift objective, and with a composition-only encoder as an ablation. Build positive and negative references within space group, exclude self-negatives, and report groups with poor reference support. Match both training compute and inference evaluations. Compare at 4, 8 and 16 denoising calls, retaining the ordinary longer sampler as a reference.
5. **Score hard samples.** Start with two distinct templates in a deliberate 80/20 mixture and report frequency error or total variation, with binomial sampling uncertainty and repeated training seeds. On MP-20, measure held-out conditional template coverage, frequency error for common classes and the fraction of missing or invalid outputs. Keep an independent metric outside the feature space used by the loss. If a full-structure claim follows, apply the same decoder and relaxation to each method. Report raw and relaxed structure matches, recovered space group, wall time and any energy criterion with its reference. Count all decode, retry, relaxation and ranking costs.

Report whether refinement improves hard samples at a fixed step budget, improves only the soft-feature metric, or performs no better than ordinary continuation. The current literature establishes the ingredients. The performance question requires a matched training experiment.
