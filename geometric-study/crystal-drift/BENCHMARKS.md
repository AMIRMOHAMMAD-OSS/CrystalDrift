# Where CrytalDrift fits among crystal generators

The [revision evidence index](revision/EVIDENCE.md) links the measured same-hardware benchmark, full public MP-20 replay, feature ablation and three WBM fits. Historical comparisons below retain their original pools and timing configurations.


**Literature checked 20 November 2026.** CrytalDrift uses the spelling proposed by the author. The timing question is whether a compact, crystal-specific drifting objective delivers useful proposals within a measured time budget. The contribution also includes an explicit pair of eight-site crystals that the implemented fingerprint maps to the same point. A broad claim to have introduced one-step crystal generation, symmetry-aware drifting, or efficient generation of plausible crystals would exceed the evidence.

## Which paper answers which question?

| Work | Mechanism, output and useful comparison |
|---|---|
| Original drifting and TokenDrift | Deng et al. move a generator distribution during training with positive and negative references, then sample in one forward pass. Oba et al. apply a detached feature target to soft-token predictions to refine a discrete diffusion language model, and report quality at fixed evaluation counts. The crystal procedure uses the first paper's one-pass sampling principle and uses analytical features of periodic geometry as its differentiable interface. [Deng et al., §§3–4 and Algorithm 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04770v2) · [TokenDrift, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19470v1) |
| SymDrift and kernel-gradient drifting | SymDrift already gives alignment and invariant-embedding approaches to symmetry-aware drifting in molecular tasks. Kernel-gradient drifting gives an intrinsic update on manifolds and results for identifiable smoothed laws under named conditions. CrytalDrift computes a two-axis-normalized field in finite Euclidean feature space and backpropagates it through a generator. Its proof has different hypotheses and a weaker conclusion. [SymDrift, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06140v3) · [Kernel-gradient drifting, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10727v2) |
| uFlowCSP and OMatG-flash | These are the closest timing precedents. uFlowCSP already predicts the complete structure from formula in one step. Its released Cartesian model is a direct empirical comparator in this study. OMatG-flash reports wall time, accuracy per number of proposals, polymorph-aware recovery, and de novo stability/uniqueness/novelty at low evaluation budgets. OMatG-flash also explicitly discusses spending saved inference time on additional samples. That idea alone provides no priority claim. [uFlowCSP](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09799v1) · [OMatG-flash, §§4.2–4.3 and Appendix F](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26402v1) |
| DiffCSP, FlowMM and CrystalFlow | Geometric baselines for composition-conditioned prediction and complete periodic geometry. FlowMM and CrystalFlow also address de novo generation. Fractional coordinates, continuous lattice charts and joint lattice/site learning are established. Our local flow is an architecture-controlled baseline. Published models have different architectures, training data and budgets. [DiffCSP](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/38b787fc530d0b31825827e2cc306656-Abstract-Conference.html) · [FlowMM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/miller24a.html) · [CrystalFlow](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-64364-4) |
| WyFormer, WyckoffDiff, Matra-Genoa, ST2M, Orbitol, DynaCrys and SymADiT | Compare these when space-group control, free orbit parameters, symmetry validity or structural diversity are the endpoints. Exact orbit and Wyckoff conditioning add information. A completed geometry, including free fractional coordinates where applicable, is needed for a geometry match or an energy evaluation. [WyFormer](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kazeev25a.html) · [WyckoffDiff](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/ekstrom-kelvinius25a.html) · [Matra-Genoa](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-025-01940-8) · [ST2M](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02906) · [Orbitol](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30086) · [DynaCrys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07401) · [SymADiT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10115) |
| MatterGen, CrystalDiT, Chemeleon2 and CrystalREPA | Relevant to discovery quality, materials priors and the cost of physical screening. MatterGen evaluates composition and structure together. CrystalDiT disambiguates the unique-and-novel fraction from the fraction also meeting a stability criterion. Chemeleon2 uses reward optimization. CrystalREPA transfers supervision from interatomic potentials. [MatterGen](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08628-5) · [CrystalDiT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16614) · [Chemeleon2](https://github.com/hspark1212/chemeleon2) · [CrystalREPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08960) |
| DiffCrysGen, CrystalGRW, DAO, EH-Diff and CrystaLLM | Other score-based, manifold, energy-assisted, hypergraph and autoregressive CIF approaches. They inform comparisons of geometry, composition validity and relaxation. Training a potential or a ranker changes the inference pipeline and its cost. [DiffCrysGen](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-026-02147-1) · [CrystalGRW](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-62470-x) · [DAO](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72362-3) · [EH-Diff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18850) · [CrystaLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04340) |
| Molecular, property-conditioned and design tasks | SALA, OrgFlow, MolCrystalFlow and OXtal condition on molecular constituents or their geometry. TFMat includes text-derived conditions, LEGO-xtal targets local environments, and OpenCSP includes high pressure. Use these as precedents for representations or measurements and disclose the extra conditioning in numerical comparisons. [SALA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34690) · [OrgFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20195) · [MolCrystalFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16020) · [OXtal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06987) · [TFMat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01076) · [LEGO-xtal](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-025-01931-9) · [OpenCSP](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-026-02180-0) |
| Non-neural controls | Retrieval and decoration of existing structures can outperform learned proposals. Each should use the same training library and reference-free donor-selection rule. Szymanski and Bartel also benchmark charge-balanced prototype enumeration and ion exchange. [Materials Horizons](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5MH00010F) |

## What to measure

| Question | Metric and protocol |
|---|---|
| Does a candidate recover a target for a given composition? | Report StructureMatcher match@1 and best-of-K at specified tolerances, plus normalized RMS distance among matched structures and the denominator. DiffCSP uses multi-candidate evaluation. Primitive reduction and isotropic volume scaling can accept a structure with the wrong volume. Our independent absolute-length score catches changes hidden by scaling. [DiffCSP study](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/38b787fc530d0b31825827e2cc306656-Paper-Conference.pdf) |
| What if multiple polymorphs share a formula? | OMatG-flash reports METRe, which searches the generated set for each compatible reference, and cRMSE, which penalizes misses. Record candidate multiplicities, reference polymorphs and whether any generated crystal can serve several references. Our reported match@K uses each target's own draws. [OMatG-flash methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26402v1) |
| How fast is it useful? | Report neural function evaluations, synchronized batch-one latency, batched throughput and matched quality versus total proposal time. Specify model size, warmup, hardware, batch, data transfers, prior sampling, relaxation, ranking and any oracle recognition. If claiming accelerated discovery, count validated, unique, novel candidates per total compute hour. [uFlowCSP](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09799v1) · [OMatG-flash](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26402v1) |
| Are raw structures valid and chemically sensible? | Classical benchmarks include structural and composition validity, coverage precision/recall in a specified fingerprint and Wasserstein discrepancies of physical properties such as density. LeMat-GenBench tests CIF parsing, minimum distance above 0.7 Å, atomic/mass density, lattice bounds, symmetry detection and oxidation-state plausibility. Our 0.5 Å contact test supplies one geometric diagnostic. [CrystalFlow validation tables](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-64364-4) · [LeMat-GenBench validity](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04562) |
| How often is a structure repeated or known? | Calculate structural uniqueness within generated samples and novelty against named, frozen reference structures. Report the matcher and chemical comparator, number drawn, denominator, and pre-/post-relaxation state. New reduced formulas and unmatched geometries count different outcomes. We report a raw structural screen against MP-20 and WBM in the supplement. [LeMat-GenBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04562) |
| How many candidates are physically competitive? | Report relaxation convergence, pathological contacts, residual forces and energy above a reference convex hull. Use consistent energy conventions and disclose relaxation and reference phases. LeMat-GenBench separates stable at or below its hull from metastable at up to 0.1 eV/atom above it. SUN is the intersection of stable, unique and novel among generated structures. State the exact energy convention and reference library. Our elemental energy gaps, matched references and force tests leave competing phases unresolved. [LeMat-GenBench stability protocol](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04562) · [MatterGen](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08628-5) |

## Data choice and comparability

- **MP-20** provides full chemistry through 20 sites in the released cells. Use the official 27,136/9,047/9,046 split for the expanded model. The original 2–8-site binary experiment is a subset, so its match rates cannot be read as full MP-20 performance. [Data release](https://github.com/httk/wyckoffdiff/blob/main/data/README.md)
- **WBM** provides an independent source of DFT-relaxed, substitution-derived structures. We selected 256,259 cells from 256,963 released records using the 20-site size cap, with 205,091 train, 25,117 validation and 26,051 test. A deterministic reduced-formula split prevents any formula from appearing in two WBM partitions. WBM was originally released in a stability-prediction setting. These splits define our own generation experiment. Prototype overlap across splits can still occur. [Matbench Discovery](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-025-01055-1) · [WBM release and attribution](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/22715158)
- **Alex-MP-20** is the natural scale-matched comparison to MatterGen and OMatG-flash. MatterGen reports 607,683 curated structures. Its chemistry, energy filtering and overlapping Materials Project ancestry change the learning problem. The archive also contains 67,521 validation structures. Our frozen MP-20 models were evaluated on 128 validation structures with Alexandria IDs and reduced formulas unseen in MP-20 training. The model fits used MP-20 and WBM. [MatterGen data curation](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08628-5)
- **Perov-5 and Carbon-24** test constrained chemistry or motifs. MPTS-52 tests larger structures and a time-based split. They answer different generalization questions from training on more crystals. A 20-slot architecture requires an explicit extension before using up to 52 sites. [DiffCSP datasets](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/38b787fc530d0b31825827e2cc306656-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **LeMat-Bulk** supplies roughly five million reference structures in LeMat-GenBench for competing phases and structural novelty. A limited WBM/MP-20 search cannot establish novelty against that larger collection. [LeMat-GenBench reference construction](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04562)

## Assessment for a preprint

The defensible advance is the specific periodic feature objective, its inexpensive crystal sampler, a precise account of what its geometry identifies, and an empirical speed/quality comparison. A literature search by method and application found one-step inorganic crystal generators and symmetry-aware drifting models. I found no primary report with this exact fingerprint, reference protocol and crystal experiment. That observation supports a narrow empirical contribution, with priority still uncertain. The original binary runs already show that a stronger released model can recover far more structures per five proposals. The potential screen also exposes severe failures. A state-of-the-art discovery or verified-stability claim would require stronger results on matched tasks and a full competing-phase evaluation.
