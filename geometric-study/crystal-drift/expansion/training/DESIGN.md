# Dedicated one-step and bank-sampler comparison

The training archive is the official MP-20 split. Compare the width-64 shuffled-anchor drift, IID-positive drift, ordinary conditional flow, and dedicated MeanFlow. Seeds are 11, 23 and 37. Each fit runs one uninterrupted AdamW trajectory, saving passes 1, 3 and 10. One pass visits 27,136 scheduled anchors and performs 1,696 updates. In the IID-positive arm all four positive record indices are sampled with replacement from the formula block. Formula labels, the schedule RNG, generated reference and query draws, and the optimizer are paired across the two drift arms. The old one-pass experiments remain separately available.

Select one checkpoint pass per training objective by the lowest mean validation fair four-feature energy score. Use the same first 128 IDs by SHA-256 of material ID, the original five-draw validation noise convention and the old train-only feature scales. Evaluate flow at sixteen steps for pass selection, then use the same pass for its truncation to one Euler step. Freeze each pass before test scoring. The official test split was previously inspected in the archived study. The checkpoint sweep uses validation only. The maximum budget is ten passes at fixed width for every arm.

The MeanFlow implementation follows Geng et al.'s identity for an average velocity in a noise-at-time-one convention. A unit inference step subtracts that velocity. We regress cell velocity in standardized log-metric coordinates and site velocity scaled by sqrt(12). The positions use within-species shuffled clean indices, coordinatewise torus lifts and a mean-free tangent. The instantaneous path velocity supplies the tangent of an exact forward-mode JVP with fixed lower time. Two independent uniform times are sorted and 25% of intervals are set to zero. A six-parameter interval embedding augments the known set backbone. We keep the compact-flow squared loss and optimizer. [Original derivation](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13447v1)

Shape errors use the norm of the traceless part of the log lattice stretch in the supplied basis. Log volume per atom gives a separate scalar error. For a placement-only diagnostic, every generated cell is assigned the reference lattice, with its generated fractional coordinates untouched, before measuring the existing four physical features. The three nearest-neighbor summaries in ångströms measure site placement without lattice error. They are insensitive to a common origin or within-species permutation and do not determine an entire motif.

The complete test panel uses ordered 20 draws on all 9,046 test IDs. Scores use the same primary RMS and maximum-site matcher calls and native-volume tolerance as the archived full-split benchmark. Each numeric report distinguishes material counts, finite geometry and matcher exceptions.

The site displacement diagnostic independently aligns predicted and reference fractional motifs in this same fixed basis. It enumerates possible anchors of the least frequent species and alternates a species-wise linear assignment with a mean periodic-residual translation. It reports the smallest feasible RMS obtained in up to four iterations per start on five proposals for each of the 128 diagnostic targets. The result upper-bounds the global minimum over all translations and species-preserving permutations.
