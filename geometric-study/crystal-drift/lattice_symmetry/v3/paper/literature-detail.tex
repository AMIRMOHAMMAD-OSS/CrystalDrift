\section{How the comparisons relate to previous generative models}
Deng et al.\ train a noise-to-sample map by attraction and repulsion and report both image quality and the evaluation count \cite{deng}. Their experiments establish the training-time mechanism. Periodicity and a predicted lattice require a different output space. SymDrift treats the symmetry of molecular conformer ensembles. In its GEOM-QM9 experiment the one-evaluation version of its ET-Flow backbone had mean recall coverage 95.7\% at 1.9 ms, compared with 96.5\% at 76.8 ms for 50-step ET-Flow \cite{symdrift}. We use the same distinction between quality at a fixed number of samples and quality at a fixed inference cost. The geometric endpoint in our experiment is recovery of a periodic crystal.

TokenDrift continues training a discrete diffusion language model and compares generative perplexity and entropy at four, eight and sixteen neural evaluations \cite{tokendrift}. Its common checkpoint and training-budget control motivated the fixed-backbone comparison here. CrytalDrift receives elemental counts and emits continuous coordinates. Drift-React takes two molecular endpoint structures and emits a reaction pathway. Its pathway geometry and inference times describe an endpoint-conditioned task \cite{driftreact}. Neither molecular timings nor token perplexities enter our MP-20 rankings.

DiffCSP diffuses the periodic sites and lattice. FlowMM uses Riemannian flow matching for the lattice and toroidal sites and studies recovery as the number of integration steps changes \cite{diffcsp,flowmm}. CrystalFlow learns a flow over a lattice representation and fractional coordinates \cite{crystalflow}. The uFlowCSP benchmark measures best-of-$k$ recovery, normalized RMS displacement, neural evaluations and the time for a 10,000-structure workload \cite{uflow}. These are the matched endpoints in Table~\ref{tab:main}. Flow matching and MeanFlow supply pretrained and independently fitted one-call or iterative controls. A supplied Wyckoff occupation in DiffCSP++ resolves a discrete part of the problem that remains unknown in our primary formula-only test \cite{diffcspplus}. Space Group Conditional Flow Matching restricts atomic motion to an initial Wyckoff occupation. Orbitol uses orbit tokens to generate the free coordinates, atom types and compatible cell for supplied occupied positions \cite{sgfm,orbitol}. OMatG-flash also studies the cost--quality trade-off of crystal generation with few-step flow maps \cite{omatg}. Here we hold the composition-inferred orbit seed fixed to measure the increment learned by periodic attraction and repulsion.
