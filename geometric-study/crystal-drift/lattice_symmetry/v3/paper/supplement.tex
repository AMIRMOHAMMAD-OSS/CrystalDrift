\section{Periodic features, finite banks and the orbit decoder}\label{app:periodic-field}

\paragraph{Objects and equivalent descriptions.}
Let \(L\in{\mathbb R}^{3\times3}\) have full rank, \(f_i\in{\mathbb R}^{1\times3}/{\mathbb Z}^3\)
and \(z_i\) be the element at site \(i\). Its physical images are \((f_i+n)L\) for integer rows \(n\).
When an orbit partition is available, \(a(i)\) denotes membership in a species-homogeneous occupied orbit.
A passive representation change, applied separately to each crystal, has the form
\[
 L'=ULQ,\qquad f'_{\pi(i)}=f_iU^{-1}+t-w_i,\qquad
 z'_{\pi(i)}=z_i,\quad a'(\pi(i))=\rho(a(i)).
\]
Here \(U\in{\rm GL}(3,{\mathbb Z})\), \(Q\in{\rm O}(3)\), \(t\) is a common fractional shift,
\(w_i\) are integer wraps and \(\rho\) renames orbit labels bijectively. Reflections are included.
A decorated structure's stabilizer comprises the isometries that map its actual decorated site set to itself.
A template selects a subgroup of that stabilizer and its occupied orbits. Transporting a template under
a setting change requires transporting all its operators and charts. Changing cell multiplicity
changes the finite motif and falls outside these assertions.

Consider a unit cubic crystal with one atom at the origin and a query equal to this reference. A raw
lattice Frobenius distance is zero. Rebase the reference by \(U=I+e_1e_2^\top\) to get distance one.
A Laplace affinity at temperature \(T\) therefore changes from one to \(\exp(-1/T)\) for the same
physical pair. Likewise, moving only a reference's coordinate origin changes an unaligned site error.

\paragraph{Common physical channels.}
For each center \(i\), enumerate all directed image vectors \(d=(f_j-f_i+n)L\) of length below
\(R=4\,\text{\AA}\), retaining every image except \(i=j,n=0\). Use
\[
 b_s(x)=\begin{cases}
 \exp\big[1-(1-x)^{-1}-\tfrac12((S-1)x-s)^2\big],&0\leq x<1,\\
 0,&x\geq1 ,
 \end{cases}\qquad s=0,\ldots,S-1,\quad S\geq2.
\]
The pair arm retains log volume, element counts, cell-translation shells and species-pair shells
of \(b_s(\|d/R\|^2)\) with \(S=2\). The order-zero arm adds the products of center-wise shell sums.
For the angular arm define coefficients \(c_{i,z,s,\ell}=\sum_{j:z_j=z,n}
b_s(\|d/R\|^2) Y_\ell(d/R)\) for real regular solid harmonics of orders \(0,1,2\).
Contract the two harmonic vectors at each center and average within central species.
All channels use sorted atomic-number ranks within an exact unit-cell formula, with seven available
species slots. Training banks compare like formulas. For the released variance arm, use the
weights \((1-\|d/R\|^2)_+^3\) in untyped center-wise harmonics, take their squared norms for each
order, average within each occupied orbit, subtract that orbit's class mean, square and average over
all occupied orbits. A class has equal element and equal orbit population in the represented cell.
The radial-resolution sensitivity retains the entire angular fingerprint. It appends
\((2N)^{-1}\sum_{i\ne j,n}b_s(\|d/R\|^2)\) in each unordered species-pair class,
with \(S=4\), where \(N\) is the number of occupied sites and both directed orientations enter
the sum. The resulting 112 entries have their own training-set normalization. The included
angular orders and their weights are unchanged. Image reindexing gives the same invariance
proof for these additional shells.

\paragraph{Typed inter-orbit contacts.}
For two different occupied orbits \(a,b\), with populations \(m_a,m_b\), define
\[
 H_{ab,s}=\frac{1}{m_am_b}\sum_{i\in a,j\in b,n}
 b_s\!\left(\frac{\|(f_j-f_i+n)L\|^2}{R^2}\right),\qquad S=3.
\]
The implementation visits both directed orientations and divides by \(2m_am_b\).
An unordered orbit pair has type consisting of its unordered elemental ranks, the indicator of
\(m_a=m_b\), and the indicator of \(\min(m_a,m_b)>1\). For each type record the means of \(H_{ab,s}\)
and \(H_{ab,s}^2\) over \emph{all} its unordered pairs. A pair with no contacts contributes zero.
An empty type contributes six zeros. This gives \(28\cdot4\cdot6=672\) extra entries.

\paragraph{Reciprocal cross intensities.}
For integer columns \(h\ne0\), set \(q=L^{-1}h\) in cycles per \(\text{\AA}\).
We include both signs of every \(h\) satisfying \(\|q\|<q_c=0.35\,\text{\AA}^{-1}\).
Write \(s_z(h)=m_z^{-1}\sum_{i:z_i=z}\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}f_i h)\), taking zero for missing species.
The three shells for each unordered pair of elements are
\[
 E_{zw,s}=\frac{1}{|\det L|(4\pi q_c^3/3)}
 \sum_{0<\|L^{-1}h\|<q_c} b_s(\|q/q_c\|^2)\,
                    {\rm Re}\big[s_z(h)\overline{s_w(h)}\big].
\]
The cutoff has no factor \(2\pi\). The denominator varies smoothly with volume, including in an
empty reciprocal sphere. Since \(h=Lq\), every included integer satisfies
\(|h_i|\leq q_c\|L_{i:}\|\). Enumerating this enclosing box followed by the physical norm test
selects a complete sphere. A fixed, basis-independent integer box generally truncates it.

\paragraph{Independent invariance.}
An integer image maps to \(n'=nU^{-1}+w_j-w_i\). This is a bijection and maps \(d\) to \(dQ\).
An orthogonal change of harmonic coordinates preserves each contracted product, including the
parity factor under reflection. Orbit-site relabelling permutes the terms in \(H\). Exchanging
the two orbits sends \(d\) to \(-d\); the class populations and both class moments survive arbitrary
renaming of equally decorated occupied orbits. Multiplicities count sites in the chosen cell.
The contact feature depends on the selected partition. For example, place two oxygen sites at
\((\pm0.1,0,0)\) in a cubic cell of side \(6\,\text{\AA}\). The two singleton orbits of a
\(P1\) template contribute a contact at \(1.2\,\text{\AA}\). The same sites make a single
orbit under inversion, giving zero inter-orbit contacts. Both templates describe identical sites
and reciprocal intensities. A subgroup change can therefore alter this feature at fixed geometry.
In reciprocal space take \(h'=Uh\). Then \((L')^{-1}h'=Q^\top q\).
Integer wraps disappear from the exponential and the common origin multiplies each \(s_z\) by
\(\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}t h')\). The conjugate cross product cancels this phase, and
\(|\det L'|=|\det L|\). Thus the raw vector of every arm is unchanged under separate
representations of any number of inputs. Subtracting fixed training vector means and dividing
each block by its fixed training RMS norm (lower bounded by \(0.01\)) preserves this conclusion.
Appending a block leaves the scaling of the common channels unchanged.

\paragraph{The stopped empirical field.}
Each scheduled training anchor defines one block at its exact formula, with two query vectors
\(x_i\) and four reference vectors \(y_j\) (two data, two generated). Repeated formulas at
different anchors retain separate banks. At each registered \(T>0\) use either
\(\ell_{ij}=-\|x_i-y_j\|/T\) or \(\ell_{ij}=-\|x_i-y_j\|^2/(2T^2)\).
The corresponding scalar affinity is \(\exp\ell_{ij}\). Normalization over the queries and the
combined reference bank is performed inside each formula block:
\[
 A_{ij}=\frac{\exp\ell_{ij}}{
   \sqrt{(\sum_u\exp\ell_{uj})(\sum_v\exp\ell_{iv})}},
\qquad m_i^\pm=\sum_{j\in\pm} A_{ij},\quad
 D_i^T=m_i^-\!\sum_{j\in+}A_{ij}y_j-m_i^+\!\sum_{j\in-}A_{ij}y_j .
\]
The opposite masses cancel the common query term had \(y_j-x_i\) been used.
With \(M\) queries across the batch, set
\[
 V_i=\frac{1}{|\mathcal T|}\sum_{T\in\mathcal T}\frac{D_i^T}{
               (10^{-8}+M^{-1}\sum_u\|D_u^T\|^2)^{1/2}},\qquad
 {\cal L}=\frac{1}{2M}\sum_i\|x_i-{\rm stop}(x_i+V_i)\|^2 .
\]
The feature-space displacement is a finite reference average. The backward pass at its
construction point is \(-M^{-1}\sum_i(\partial_\theta x_i)^\top V_i\). No derivative of an
affinity or a reference enters this expression. A fractional-coordinate covector at fixed
lattice satisfies \(g_f=g_x L^\top\), whereas the network's physical velocity has units of
length. The scalar potential, feature vector and Cartesian velocity play distinct roles.
Each field, stopped loss and its parameter derivative are unchanged if all inputs are independently
re-expressed and the generator obeys the paired-seed map below. Differentiability is required for
the gradient assertion. Smooth radial envelopes remove first-derivative jumps at the cutoffs.

\paragraph{Finite-bank equilibrium.}
Conditional on all queries, fixed model parameters and conditioning labels, suppose that
exchanging the entire two equal-size signed banks preserves their joint law, simultaneously in
all blocks entering the common RMS. For example, both banks may consist of independent draws from
the same conditional feature law, independent of the queries. Swapping their signs permutes
columns of \(A\), exchanges \(m^+,m^-\), negates every \(D^T\) and preserves its denominator.
The field is bounded by \(\sqrt M\) per query, so antisymmetry gives
\(\mathbb E[V_i\mid x,\mathrm{labels}]=0\). A realized finite bank can have nonzero field.
The actual coverage schedule fixes a first positive by training row and samples one additional
exact-composition positive. Two generated references use separate template/geometry draws.
Conditioning on the fixed positive generally destroys exchangeability. Reusing a finite
seed/positive cache, without-replacement scheduling across groups and exclusions tied to
the anchor likewise require the joint-law hypothesis to be checked before claiming a stochastic
fixed point. For example, take two identical scalar zero queries, data and generator laws
uniform on \(\{-1,+1\}\), two negative draws and a first positive fixed to \(+1\). Enumerating
the eight choices for the other positive and the two negatives gives mean field \(0.3750\)
to four decimals for both affinities. Fixing the first positive to \(-1\) reverses this value.
Averaging the two anchors recovers zero in this example.

In the first training traversal, the second positive had a different material ID
from its anchor on 2,085 occasions. The raw angular vectors differed in all
2,085 of these pairs at an \(\ell_2\) threshold of \(10^{-8}\). The appended
contact and reciprocal vectors differed in 2,030 and 2,071 pairs, respectively.
At the frozen small angular checkpoint, we applied each loss to the same
first 128 training anchors and their saved queries and negative draws.
The mean effective negative counts were 1.80, 1.79 and 1.79 for angular,
contact and reciprocal features, respectively. The corresponding median
per-query fractional-coordinate gradient norms in the stored native charts were 2.20, 2.13 and 2.26
after undoing the loss's mean over queries. These measurements establish a
feature and gradient response on the sampled records. The matched validation
and test experiments measure whether that response improves structural recovery.

\paragraph{Orbit-token map.}
The prior returns compatible \(L,F\) and fractional chart derivatives \(J_i\) such that an
orbit-parameter increment \(du_a\) changes row \(f_i\) by \(du_a J_i\).
Chemical features, distances, multiplicities, chart ranks, log volume per site, atom count and
conditioning times are scalars. The network averages physical-neighbor messages at sites and
pools site tokens within each occupied orbit. Its residual token layers communicate through a
multiplicity-weighted global mean in the global variant. All scalar edge outputs are unchanged by
the paired representation and both token and site orders may be permuted. Let \(w_{ij,n}\) be
the cutoff envelope divided by one plus its sum for center \(i\), and let the signed edge
coefficients \(\alpha,\beta\) include a hyperbolic tangent. With \(D_i=J_iL\) and
\(H_a=\sum_{i\in a}D_iD_i^\top\), the site prediction is
\[
 v_i=\sum_{j,n}w_{ij,n}\alpha_{ij,n}d_{ij,n},\quad
 du_a=\sum_{i\in a}v_iD_i^\top H_a^+,\quad
 f_i^{\rm out}=f_i+du_{a(i)}J_i\pmod1 .
\]
The Moore--Penrose inverse solves least squares over all images of an occupied orbit. Fixed charts
have zero displacement. Under \(J'_{\pi(i)}=J_iU^{-1}\) one has \(D'=DQ\), \(H'=H\),
\(v'=vQ\) and \(du'=du\). Any invertible reparameterization of the free chart preserves the
range of simultaneous attainable site displacements. Orthogonal projection onto that range is
unique, including for deficient rank, so each physical output is unchanged. The numerical
pseudoinverse and rank routines have explicit tolerances. Tests use well-separated ranks.
The projection is nonexpansive in the stacked physical coordinates. Since
\(\|v_i\|\leq\sum w_{ij,n}|\alpha_{ij,n}|\,\|d_{ij,n}\|<R\), an orbit of multiplicity \(m_a\)
satisfies \(\sum_{i\in a}\|du_aJ_iL\|^2<m_a R^2\). This bounds a single update in the
seed lattice. It places no bound on distance to a reference structure after matching.

For \(N\) occupied sites the deformation is
\[
 B=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{ij,n}w_{ij,n}\beta_{ij,n}\,\frac{d_{ij,n}^\top d_{ij,n}}{R^2},
 \quad A=I+0.3 B,\qquad
 L_{\rm out}= L\,\frac{A}{\det(A)^{1/3}}\exp(\delta/3),
\]
where \(\delta=0.6\tanh(\text{volume head})\). Since \(\sum_{jn}w_{ij,n}<1\),
\(|\beta|\leq1\) and \(\|d\|<R\), the symmetric \(B\) has operator norm below one.
The eigenvalues of \(A\) lie in \((0.7,1.3)\). Its positive determinant makes the shape
normalization well defined, \(L_{\rm out}L_{\rm out}^\top\) is positive definite and
\(\log(V_{\rm out}/V_{\rm in})=\delta\).
Transport sends \(B\) to \(Q^\top BQ\) and leaves \(\delta\) unchanged. Therefore
\(L'_{\rm out}=U L_{\rm out}Q\) and \(f'^{\rm out}_{\pi(i)}=f_i^{\rm out}U^{-1}+t\bmod1\).
If a seed operation preserves its species and occupied chart, it permutes equivalent rows in this
calculation. A shared increment updates all affine images of an orbit. The transformed cell
remains compatible with every requested operation. Additional symmetry, specializations and
collisions are possible. Exact equality of a detected group is an empirical statement.

For a seed law \(\mu_c\) at supplied composition \(c\), \(G_\#\mu_c\) is the output law.
Pathwise equivariance gives an equivariant pushforward when seeds, charts and noise are
transported consistently. Drawing a fresh canonical prior directly in an arbitrary cell setting
requires a separate distributional statement. The matched MeanFlow control uses canonical
endpoint assignment and lattice-matrix regression; the independent-bank theorem concerns drifting.

\paragraph{Matched one-step MeanFlow control.}
The shared-backbone control retains the same seed \(X_1=(L,F)\), orbit derivatives and
chemistry. In the canonical training chart, assign each observed site to a compatible
seed species by minimum-cost bipartite matching of wrapped fractional chords measured in
the seed metric. Project the assigned Cartesian chords onto the occupied-orbit tangents
to obtain \(\Delta_F\). Average the observed cell metric over the inferred seed group
and restore its observed volume to obtain \(H_\star\). The cell endpoint offset is
\(\Delta_L=L[(L^{-1}H_\star L^{-\top})^{1/2}-I]\), with an eigenvalue
floor of \(10^{-10}\) before taking the square root. The root is symmetric positive
definite, so its linear interpolation with the identity gives nonsingular intermediate
cells. These endpoints are the projected training pairs, including cases where the
observed occupied template differs from the seed's.

For \(\Delta=(\Delta_L,\Delta_F)\), use \(X_t=X_1+(1-t)\Delta\). At fixed lower time
\(r\), the backward average rate \(u=(X_r-X_t)/(t-r)\) obeys
\(u+(t-r)D_tu=-\dot X_t\), where
\(D_tu=\partial_tu+(\partial_Xu)\dot X_t\). Along the paired path
\(\dot X_t=-\Delta\). The implemented regression target is
\(\widehat u={\rm stop}[\Delta-(t-r)D_tu_\theta(X_t,r,t)]\).
The sign follows by differentiating \((t-r)u=X_r-X_t\). The Jacobian--vector
product holds seed chart parameters and \(r\) fixed. Let \(e_L,e_F\) be the
lattice and fractional components of \(u_\theta-\widehat u\). With lengths in
angstroms the objective is half the sum of the mean of all entries of \((e_L/4)^2\)
and the per-structure mean of \(\|e_F L/4\|_F^2/(3N)\). Fractional site padding
has zero displacement. In training, \(t\) is the greater of two independent uniform
draws on \([0,1]\). Set \(r=t\) with probability \(0.30\) and otherwise take \(r\)
as the smaller draw. The sampled operation at inference uses \(t=1,r=0\).
Training uses both the directional derivative and a live forward for regression.
The one-call count refers to generation.

\paragraph{Identifiability tests.}
Finite-order invariant descriptors can agree for inequivalent atomic environments
\cite{degenerate}. The following examples test the actual cutoffs, species channels
and orbit partitions used by our periodic field.
In a \(50\times5\times5\,\text{\AA}^3\) orthogonal cell put four identical sites along the long
axis at twelfths \(A=\{0,1,4,6\}\) or \(B=\{0,1,3,7\}\). The shortest lattice vectors span the
transverse plane, so an isometry between the two crystals could only preserve or reverse their
circular long-axis gaps. Those gap multisets are respectively \(\{1,2,3,6\}\) and
\(\{1,2,4,5\}\). Nevertheless, every undirected circular pair distance \(1,\ldots,6\) occurs once
in each motif. Consequently \(|s(h)|^2\), the Fourier transform of the ordered difference
multiset, agrees for every reciprocal index. All local edges are absent within \(4\,\text{\AA}\).
Using singleton orbits, every arm therefore assigns both structures the same vector.
Point masses on these two structures have equal feature laws and zero field despite different
geometry. The amplitudes also discard a common origin phase and the real contractions identify
reflected environments. An application treating enantiomers as distinct modulo proper
rotations would need a chirality-sensitive observable. Neither a characteristic affinity on raw crystals nor a convergence,
uniqueness or thermodynamic-stability theorem follows.

\paragraph{The earlier eight-site witness.}
For the fixed-basis precursor, place four oxygen sites at \((s/12,0,0)\) for
\(s\in A\) or \(s\in B\) above, and four silicon sites at their common offset
\((0,1/3,0)\) in a unit cubic cell. The sets are a classical homometric pair
\cite{zrelation,homometry}. The oxygen motif occupies a single coordinate circle
in either cell. A cubic lattice isometry preserves the occupied axis up to sign.
Its distinct circular gap multisets exclude such an isometry.
The ordered difference counts are four at residue zero, two at six, one elsewhere.
Consequently the oxygen powers agree at every integer wave. Silicon's amplitude is
the oxygen amplitude times \(\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}h_2/3)\), the same phase in both
structures. Each trained mixed triple probes \(e_i,e_j,e_i+e_j\) with \(i\ne j\).
At most one of the first two waves probes the variable axial motif, and the third
then returns its conjugate. The 70-entry binary vector therefore agrees. In the
1,306-entry multielement precursor, the same-species auto-power and both complex
cross-powers to the whole-motif and first-species anchors also depend on the motif
only through its common power.

In contrast, with \(a_z(h)\) the species mean amplitude, substitution of the
twelfth roots of unity gives
\[
a_{\rm O}(e_1)^2\overline{a_{\rm Si}(2e_1)}
 = ( -\sqrt{3}+\mathrm{i})/32\quad\text{or}\quad(\sqrt{3}+\mathrm{i})/32.
\]
The difference of the scaled real parts is \(1/(16\sqrt2)\).
Replaying both original implementations gives maximum channel differences of
\(4.2\times10^{-17}\) and \(6.9\times10^{-18}\), and \(0.04419417\) for the
extra channel. A fixed axial frequency needs to travel with the basis for this
calculation to describe the same observable. In the present one-angstrom fixture the
physical reciprocal sphere is empty, but the present local power blocks of orders
zero, one and two differ in unscaled Euclidean norm by \(0.00246\), \(0.01381\)
and \(0.00647\). They already distinguish these two particular decorated structures.

Now fix transverse periods at \(5\,\text{\AA}\) and vary the axial period \(a\)
without changing the fractional motifs. When \(a\geq48\,\text{\AA}\), the
smallest nonzero circular axial separation, \(a/12\), reaches the strict local
cutoff. The four oxygen--silicon columns then have identical disconnected local
neighborhoods in the two structures. This gives a sufficient condition for
collision. Give each occupied site its own orbit in
the supplied \(P1\) chart. For each element all four singleton orbits have
equal center powers, so the variance across those orbits vanishes. All local descriptors,
including the denser radial shells and the first two contact moments, coincide
between the two. The reciprocal cross channels coincide for every \(a\) by
the power argument above. In a sweep of 57 axial periods the largest unscaled
block difference for \(a\geq48\,\text{\AA}\) was below \(8\times10^{-17}\).
