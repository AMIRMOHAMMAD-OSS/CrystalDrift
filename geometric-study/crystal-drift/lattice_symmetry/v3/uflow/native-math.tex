\paragraph{Native Gaussian control for the published architecture.}
The released Cartesian sampler draws each of its twelve scalar channels independently
at each of 100 padded tokens. For one channel let $g_a\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$ independently
and $e_a=g_a-\bar g$. With $n$ occupied sites, each of the nine decoded cell entries
for the zero-call control is $n^{-1}\sum_{a=1}^{n}e_a$. Its variance is
$\sigma_n^2=n^{-1}-100^{-1}$, including the covariance between $\bar g$
and the occupied-token mean. The nine entries remain independent across channels.
Writing $L=\sigma_n A$ for a standard normal $3\times3$ matrix, columnwise
orthogonalization gives $|\det A|$ as the product of independent $\chi_3,\chi_2,\chi_1$
variables. Hence
\[
 \mathbb E[|\det L|\mid n]=2\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\sigma_n^3,
 \qquad \mathrm{Var}(|\det L|\mid n)=(6-8/\pi)\sigma_n^6.
\]
For 12 sites the predicted mean is $0.03169$~\AA$^3$ and the observed mean is
$0.03158$~\AA$^3$. Among the 180,920 native prior draws on the 9,046 official
formulas, 141,900 had positive volume below $0.1$~\AA$^3$.
All site-count-specific means were within 2.29 theoretical standard errors.
These proposals remain in the scale-enabled and native-volume denominators.

\paragraph{Published average-velocity control.}
Let $x$ contain the occupied Cartesian coordinates and repeated lattice entries,
$z=x+t(e-x)$, and $v=e-x$. The official transport draws $0<r\le t<1$.
One quarter of each minibatch receives $r=t$ (rounded down).
With probability $0.8$ it sets $v_*=v$ and uses a null crystal system.
Other samples receive $v_*=1.5v-0.5u_{\mathrm{EMA}}(z,t,t,\varnothing)$.
In both cases let $D u=J_z u\,v_*+\partial_tu$ at fixed $r$ and sampled context.
For sample $b$, the squared error $d_b$ is the mean over its occupied tokens and
twelve output channels of
$[u-\mathrm{stop}(v_*-(t-r)Du)]^2$. The batch loss averages
$d_b\,\mathrm{stop}[(d_b+10^{-3})^{-1}]$. The reference implementation
computes the directional derivative with \texttt{torch.func.jvp}. An independent
affine-velocity calculation on twelve masked 100-token examples reproduced its loss,
unweighted mean squared error and stopped parameter gradient (0.10893675045).
At inference the network receives the supplied atom counts and null system and
returns $z-u(z,1,0)$ in one forward evaluation. The constrained periodic equivariance
theorems for CrytalDrift apply to the corresponding geometric decoder.

The Cartesian control first sorts the supplied species multiset by electronegativity
and atomic number. For fixed ordered noise this preprocessing gives the same input
tokens, hence the same decoded arrays, for every presentation of the formula.
Inside the transformer a position code indexed by the cumulative valid-token count
is added before attention. Exchanging two equal species and their noise vectors
therefore need not commute with the learned update. Likewise a generic learned
linear projection of Cartesian components need not commute with a simultaneous
rotation of the coordinate and lattice rows. On two fixed formula/noise fixtures,
both eight-pass EMAs reproduced reordered-formula outputs exactly. Their largest
velocity discrepancies after exchanging equal-species noise vectors were $0.787$
and $0.984$ for the small and medium networks. Under a fixed Cartesian quarter-turn
the discrepancies were $4.700$ and $4.629$ in native output units. These comparisons
concern pointwise commutation at fixed coupled noise.
