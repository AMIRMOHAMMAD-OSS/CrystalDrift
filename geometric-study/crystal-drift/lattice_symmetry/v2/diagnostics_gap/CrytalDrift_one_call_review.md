# CrytalDrift one-call recovery audit

## Question

The released periodic CrytalDrift and uFlowCSP both use one neural evaluation per proposed crystal. On the official MP-20 test split and 20 proposals per target their recovery rates are 31.79% and 79.32%. The latter is the local replay of the released checkpoint. This audit examines where those 47.53 percentage points arise, whether changing CrytalDrift can help while preserving one-call inference, and what the Wyckoff experiments establish.

## Released benchmark and diagnosis

The crosswalk uses the same 9,046 targets, exact unit-cell species counts, draw budgets and pinned pymatgen StructureMatcher. The uFlow replay uses its null space-group condition. The test IDs match the external authors’ test list and are disjoint from their train list. Figures below average three frozen CrytalDrift fits and compare one released uFlow checkpoint. A match rate at 20 draws counts a target if at least one of its first 20 proposals matches. Pymatgen's usual scale-enabled recovery allows volume rescaling. The additional native-volume criterion requires a matched proposal to lie within a factor 1.1 of the target volume. The interval for the full 20-draw gap is 46.55–48.51 percentage points, bootstrapping target IDs conditional on these fits.

| Test group | Targets | Angular at 20 draws | uFlowCSP at 20 draws |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1–4 sites | 1,852 | 91.50% | 95.36% |
| 5–8 sites | 2,085 | 48.76% | 91.37% |
| 9–12 sites | 2,193 | 6.19% | 83.77% |
| 13–20 sites | 2,916 | 0.97% | 57.17% |
| All | 9,046 | 31.79% | 79.32% |

At one draw the rates are 6.83% and 46.32%. At five they are 19.68% and 68.05%. Cells containing at least nine sites explain 77.7% of the net 20-draw deficit. On 7,833 targets whose exact composition is absent from training, the rates are 33.10% and 82.54%. Among 2,744 targets recovered by both checkpoints at seed 11, the average of the best normalized RMS displacements is about 0.342 for CrytalDrift and 0.033 for uFlow. The discrepancy includes the quality of recovered geometries.

**What one pass actually computes.** The released CrytalDrift physical generator has 6,723 parameters, a width of 24, and local edge messages at a 4 Å cutoff. Its learned per-site motions refine initially uniform fractional site positions. Its initial distribution of cell shapes depends on atom count, while its initial volume also depends on composition. The matched full-data prior used all 27,136 training structures. The selected angular generator was trained for 256 updates (~2,350 distinct positive structures per fit). The released uFlowCSP EMA checkpoint has 131,215,116 parameters in a 12-layer, 768-wide chemistry-aware transformer. It learns a mean-flow map using chemical features and a training-only crystal-system signal. The training configuration and architecture differ substantially. Their individual contributions to the performance gap cannot be read off this comparison. [uFlowCSP paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09799) · [Released weights](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.33974422.v1)

**What CrytalDrift actually changes.** On all 542,760 model draws, the ratio between the largest and smallest principal stretch of the *deformation from seed to output* was below 1.01. The angular head mostly dilates the seed, with median volume increases of 11.4–15.9% across the three fits. The model permits an anisotropic strain by construction. The trained weights make little use of it.

On 128 preselected validation IDs, five draws and three trained seeds, the original models recovered 17.71% under the scale-enabled criterion. Giving them the true volume alone left recovery at 17.71%. Replacing the entire cell with the known cell gave 29.43%. Replacing the fractional sites with the known sites gave 38.02%. Replacing both gave 100%. These are counterfactual diagnostics in the stored basis with no deployable use of target information. An independent component swap on 512 test IDs also found that transferring only the lattice or only the motif from uFlow failed to reproduce the recovery of its jointly generated structure.

## Which intended ideas the release actually tested

| Idea | What the study established |
|---|---|
| Invariant training drift and equivariant generator | Finite-batch proof and numerical tests for independent changes of lattice basis, Cartesian frame, periodic origin, wrapping and atom order. The generator jointly updates the lattice and atom sites in one call. |
| Species-resolved spherical-harmonic features | Yes. Local powers through order 2 reach 31.79% at 20 draws. Pair shells reach 31.30% and center-wise order-zero powers reach 31.47%. Relative to order zero, directional orders add 0.32 percentage points (paired 95% interval 0.15–0.49). Both example collisions were already separated by center-wise order zero. |
| Given group and occupied Wyckoff orbits | Yes. The model supports general and special positions using a compatible lattice and orbit expansion. On all 9,046 test IDs, one draw from the template-conditioned angular model gives 50.29%, versus 45.90% from the same supplied-template prior. The target supplies its element-labelled Wyckoff template here. |
| Extra Wyckoff-orbit features in the drift affinity | Yes. The orbit-C ablation adds three continuous features measuring between-orbit variability within each element/multiplicity class. The same 106/128 targets were recovered at 20 draws with and without that block. It can contribute a geometry-dependent signal for only 3,556/9,039 accepted test templates. Even among the 52 eligible targets in the ablation panel, the same 30 were recovered. This particular variant has no demonstrated incremental recovery gain on that panel. |
| Inferring symmetry from composition | The 50.29% headline did not test this ability. Our new symbolic-transfer experiment below chooses candidate groups and Wyckoff letters from training data using the target's elemental counts alone. It tests a retrieval-assisted prior. A learned joint distribution over compositions, space groups and occupied orbits for de novo discovery is still a separate experiment. |

## Controlled changes that keep one neural evaluation

- **Volume calibration.** A training-only elemental-pair volume readout keeps the predicted shape and sites. A direct paired rematch of 1,920 validation draws increased native-volume match at five draws from 9.38% to 15.36%, a gain of 5.99 percentage points (paired 95% interval 3.13–9.38). No scale-enabled match flag changed in that validation rematch. Applying the calibrated volume to the stored match flags on all 9,046 test IDs raised native-volume recovery at 20 draws from 21.24% to 27.94% (paired improvement 6.71 points). The latter is a sensitivity analysis on frozen scale-enabled outcomes.
- **Training and displacement magnitude.** On the same 128 validation IDs and three seeds, actual five-draw match rates at 256, 1,024 and 3,392 updates were 17.71%, 15.89% and 15.36%. Continuing this optimizer schedule did not help. On 192 other validation targets, doubling only the unwrapped Cartesian site displacement gave 34 instead of 32 matches at five draws (paired 95% interval for the gain −2.08 to +4.17 points). Native-volume matches stayed at 17/192. Increasing a step size alone has little support from this test.
- **Transferred geometry seeds.** Draw up to ten distinct compatible training prototypes ranked by chemical proximity and alternate them with the original random seeds across 20 proposals. Assign the requested elements and adjust donor volume from a training-only composition model. Apply the frozen angular checkpoint once. The final version preserves the composition-adjusted volume on donor slots. The comparison includes the identical seeds without applying a network. On the independent 128-target validation confirmation, scaled hits were 40 for the original angular, 99 for mixed seeds alone and 101 for mixed seeds plus the network. Native-volume hits were respectively 25, 94 and 93. The improvement over random seeding came predominantly from transferring a plausible structure.
- **Transferred symbolic templates.** Choose candidate groups and element-labelled occupied Wyckoff positions from training analogues by stoichiometry and chemical proximity. Sample free coordinates and a compatible lattice with the train-fitted prior, then use the existing template-trained angular checkpoint once. The template transfers group and letter patterns. Free coordinates are sampled afresh, and the geometric prior draws on training-set cell shapes. The query supplies only the unit-cell composition. On 192 validation targets at five draws, standard angular recovered 31, the symbolic prior recovered 92, and symbolic prior plus one angular call recovered 105. The learned increment over the same symbolic seeds was 6.77 points (paired 95% interval 3.13–10.94). These comparisons use the formula benchmark's proposal-first StructureMatcher convention.


## Formula-only comparison on the same 384 official test targets

Each arm receives the unit-cell element counts. Donor IDs come exclusively from the training split. A salted hash fixed all 384 target IDs independently of the new arms' scores. The original release had already evaluated the official test split. The five-draw symbolic experiment and the mixed-geometry comparison were specified before evaluating those arms on these targets. The symbolic experiment's 20-draw extension followed inspection of its five-draw result. The first five draws are identical in both analyses. Four of 7,680 symbolic proposal pairs failed conversion or verification and count as failures. The neural arms use one frozen model evaluation per proposal. Selecting a donor, sampling a template and expanding its coordinates cost wall time even in the zero-call controls.

| Method | Neural calls per draw | Recovery, 1 draw | Recovery, 5 draws | Recovery, 20 draws | Native-volume recovery, 20 draws |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Released formula-only angular, mean of three fits | 1 | 6.51% | 17.97% | 30.82% | 20.40% |
| Predicted Wyckoff templates alone | 0 | 38.02% | 54.69% | 60.94% | 54.69% |
| Predicted templates + template-trained angular | 1 | 42.19% | 57.55% | 64.84% | 46.61% |
| Mixed geometry seeds alone | 0 | 61.46% | 73.70% | 82.55% | 76.56% |
| Mixed geometry seeds + angular, donor volume preserved | 1 | 61.72% | 73.70% | 82.55% | 77.08% |
| Released uFlowCSP | 1 | 46.88% | 66.15% | 79.17% | 77.34% |

**Symbolic Wyckoff templates.** At five draws the frozen template-trained generator adds 11 recovered targets beyond the 210 recovered by exactly the same symbolic seeds. Its paired gain is 2.86 percentage points (95% interval 0.78–5.21). At 20 draws the learned gain is 15 targets or 3.91 points (1.82–5.99). It still trails uFlow by 14.32 points at 20 draws (9.90–18.75). At five draws among 231 targets with at least nine sites, the symbolic prior recovers 91, the refined prior 98 and uFlow 126. The released angular models average 1.33 recovered targets. Among 333 targets with unseen exact compositions, the symbolic angular method recovers 200 at five draws and uFlow recovers 231. Previously unseen compositions can still share a trained structural prototype.

Using a deterministic rescaling after the angular prediction to preserve the symbolic seed's sampled volume increases native-volume recovery from 156 to 191 at five draws and from 179 to 224 at 20 (58.33%). Adding this correction requires no target volume and no further network call. At 20 draws its paired improvement over the uncorrected native-volume endpoint is 11.72 points (8.33–15.36). This is an exploratory adjustment to the frozen predictions. The pinned standard matcher produced identical match flags on all 2,360 rescaled proposals checked, including the entire valid five-draw prefix. The 20-draw sensitivity uses the original scale-enabled flags for the remaining candidates.

**Transferred geometries.** Transferring donor fractional coordinates and cell shape is a stronger use of the training set. With 20 draws, the volume-preserving angular variant recovered 317/384 versus 304 for uFlow, a paired difference of +3.39 points (−0.78 to +7.55). On the native-volume endpoint the counts are 296 versus 297. Removing the angular model but retaining identical mixed geometry seeds still recovers 317 (three gained and three lost relative to refinement). The paired learned increment is 0.00 points (−1.30 to +1.30). Among the 231 targets with at least nine sites, the mixed seeds alone and the one-call model both recovered 178, compared with eight for the original seed-11 angular checkpoint and 160 for uFlow. The data support transfer of known geometric motifs as a strong formula-conditioned baseline. They provide no evidence that the learned orbit kernel independently closes the gap to uFlow.

**Timing the symbolic route.** With an indexed training set in memory, one CPU thread, batch size one, 128 interleaved warm targets and a new donor search per formula, median first-proposal latency was 2.09 ms for the original angular model, 1.24 ms for the symbolic prior and 3.08 ms for the symbolic prior plus its one angular call. These times exclude offline indexing and retrospective structural matching. Hardware contention and different run protocols limit comparisons with the manuscript's separate clocks. A time-budget advantage over uFlow would need a simultaneous matched replay.

**Direct test of the orbit-aware affinity on inferred templates.** I replayed the standard angular checkpoint and two orbit-aware checkpoints on the exact 384 targets and five ordered symbolic seeds used above. The ordinary angular model and the validation-selected orbit model (512 updates) each recovered 221/384. One target was unique to each, giving a paired difference of 0.00 points (95% interval −0.78 to +0.78). At the matched 256 training updates, the orbit and ordinary angular models recovered exactly the same 221 targets. All three improved the normalized score over the zero-call symbolic prior (210 recovered). Under the native-volume criterion the ordinary angular model recovered 156, the selected orbit variant 120 and the 256-update orbit variant 89, versus 182 for the symbolic prior. The selected orbit model loses 9.38 points relative to angular under the native-volume criterion (95% interval −12.76 to −5.99). Median unrelaxed volume inflation relative to target was about 13.4% for the selected orbit model and 7.6% for ordinary angular. This five-draw ablation was run after seeing the symbolic experiment, uses frozen checkpoints, and tests one specific orbit descriptor.


In a separate matched CPU run on 48 formulas, the first fully decoded proposal took a median of 2.05 ms for original angular, 2.41 ms with mixed geometry seeds, angular refinement and donor-volume anchoring, and 0.63 ms for mixed seeds alone. Generating 20 serial candidates took 32.08, 32.88 and 0.79 ms, respectively. Each formula required a fresh donor search. The one-time training-index build took 0.35 s. These local, warm, batch-one clocks exclude CIF serialization, relaxation and matching.

## What to train next

1. Use the symbolic Wyckoff route for the next scientific comparison. It can choose an occupied orbit pattern from the unit-cell formula, sample fresh free coordinates, and show a measurable learned improvement over its own prior. Report symbolic transfer and full-geometry transfer as distinct baselines. A full-geometry donor can already solve many targets without any learned continuous update.
2. Train the continuous generator on the seeds it will receive at inference, including plausible alternative templates that can be drawn from composition. Give the network global chemical context across occupied orbits. predict anisotropic symmetry-compatible cell changes and updates to each orbit's free parameters in a single forward pass. Keep the current finite-bank drift with the same symmetry-compatible seed law for an objective ablation. If including a *learned* template selector, count its inference call too. Integrate template logits and conditional geometry in one model invocation or retain the deterministic lookup to claim a single neural evaluation end to end.
3. Use a disjoint validation panel to select checkpoints on five-draw recovery, native-volume recovery and larger cells. Compare the same initial seeds, neural architecture, training examples, update budget and random seeds with (a) no neural update, (b) ordinary angular drift and (c) an explicitly orbit-resolved drift. A descriptor that carries typed cross-orbit distances or neighbor geometry between distinct orbits would address information discarded by the current three variance channels. Require a measured improvement in those exact matched controls before claiming a benefit from a new kernel. Measure GPU/CPU time per proposal and matches per wall-clock budget including template search and decoding.
4. After freezing the model and selection rule, evaluate all 9,046 MP-20 test IDs, multiple training seeds, and a separate split holding out structure-prototype families from the donor inventory and model training. Use sealed comparisons with the original, fitted prior, independent one-step predictor and budget-matched iterative model. Compute ordinary and native-volume recovery, RMS displacement, per-size results and paired target-level uncertainty. The diagnostic subset has already informed research decisions.
5. De novo generation requires separate evidence. Sample the composition and symmetry variables without using a held-out target, report the origin of both, and measure validity, uniqueness and novelty after checking training-set matches, plus stability under the prespecified relaxation and hull protocol. Any discovery or competitive unattended-sampling claim requires this evaluation. The present intervention concerns formula-conditioned structure recovery.

## Reproduction

Extract the two research archives linked in the 21-page release and overlay the diagnostic archive on the crystal-drift project root. All new paths are under `lattice_symmetry/v2/`. Use the release's CPU Torch environment for learned forward passes and its pinned pymatgen 2024.4.13 matcher environment for recovery. The audit figure uses Matplotlib and reads the saved CSV/JSON results.

All new proposals are evaluated without relaxation for structure-prediction recovery. The supplied-template protocol in the manuscript uses its documented matcher argument order. The new formula-only experiments consistently use proposal-first matching. Numerical differences across these two protocols should be interpreted with this distinction in mind.
