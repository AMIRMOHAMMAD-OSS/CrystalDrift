\section{Independent banks, their weak limit, and de novo sampling}\label{s:weak-limit}
Let $\mu_d=\Phi_\#\widehat P_d$ be the reference feature law for an observed cell composition $d$. Let $\nu_d=\Phi_\# Q_{\theta,d}$ be the corresponding generated feature law. In the original multielement fit, a shuffle of the deposited training structures selects an anchor and thereby fixes the composition of a block. The anchor itself occupies the first positive slot. Conditional on its identity and the training history, this positive is fixed. The remaining positives are sampled independently from its composition group. This distinction survives even if the generator reproduces the conditional law exactly.

\claim{Proposition 5 (independent positives and negatives)}{Fix the archive, model parameters, query features, finite composition schedule and training history through the previous update. Model the fresh bank draws as independent random variables. Sample \emph{every} positive independently with replacement from the scheduled empirical group, including the first. Sample each negative independently from the generator at the corresponding composition, with noise independent of positives and queries. If $\nu_d=\mu_d$ at the scheduled compositions, the conditional expectation of every normalized query drift is zero.}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} Enumerate slots by $j$ and write $d_j$ for their fixed labels. The positive array has product law $\bigotimes_j\mu_{d_j}$ and the negative array has product law $\bigotimes_j\nu_{d_j}$. Independence makes their joint law exchangeable under simultaneous bank exchange at equality. The exchange reverses every numerator in \eqref{s:force} and preserves the batch scale in \eqref{s:rms}, even when several blocks share a composition. Taking the conditional expectation gives zero. The argument also conditions on the live query Jacobians if their inputs are fixed. The shuffled schedule may depend on the training history. The new positive indices and the independent random inputs retain the specified conditional law after conditioning on that schedule. \proofend

\claim{Proposition 6 (weak approximation at fixed queries)}{Keep the same query features, condition labels, finite bank sizes, strictly positive temperatures and RMS regularizer $\epsilon=10^{-8}$. If $\nu_d^{(m)}\Rightarrow\mu_d$ for each scheduled composition, then $\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathbb E[V_i^{(m)}]=0$ for every query $i$.}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} The features live in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space. The negative array converges weakly to $\bigotimes_j\mu_{d_j}$ by finite product convergence. Adjoining the independent positive array preserves weak convergence. For fixed queries, Euclidean distance is continuous, including at coincident points. Log-softmax and exponentiation are continuous for finite inputs. Sums, products, and the weighted centroid differences preserve continuity. Finally $s_\tau\geq\sqrt\epsilon>0$, so the complete map on the two arrays is continuous everywhere. If there are $B$ queries in the batch, then
\[
   \|D_i^\tau\|^2 \leq B(s_\tau^2-\epsilon)
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \|V_i\|\leq\sqrt B .
\]
Every scalar coordinate of $V_i$ is therefore a bounded continuous test function. Its expectation converges to the expectation under independent equal-law reference arrays, which vanishes by Proposition 5. No moment assumption on individual bank features is needed. \proofend

The regularizer makes the zero-field batch a continuity point. Differentiability of the norm at zero plays no role. The result concerns the real-valued formula implemented by the finite-precision routines. It fixes the query features and the number of references as the conditional laws vary. It gives neither a rate for the empirical optimizer nor a converse guarantee from vanishing drift. At finite weights the continuous noise-to-cell map can have support outside the finite archive; distinct structural laws can also have the same descriptor distribution by Proposition 3 and Section~\ref{s:ablation}.

\paragraph{An exhaustive finite-bank check.}
We selected two distinct deposited structures of one composition, computed their 1,306 features, and fixed two queries on their affine span. Each positive and negative bank has two independent slots drawing from the equally weighted pair. Enumerating all sixteen bank pairs exactly integrates the implemented direction. Moving both support points of the negative law by $\delta$ in the first lattice coordinate gives a family with the stated weak limit. At $\delta=10^{-7},10^{-8},0$, the larger of the two expected-query norms was $2.05\times10^{-4}$, $2.05\times10^{-5}$ and $4.40\times10^{-17}$. Fixing the first positive at one support point yielded 0.4503 at equality. Across the sweep the maximum bank-swap residual was $2.78\times10^{-13}$ and the largest observed query norm was 1.375, below $\sqrt 2$. The measured fields accompany the proof in \work{expansion/weak-limit.json}.

\paragraph{Which distribution is being measured?}
Write $\mathcal C$ for the supported integer element multisets and $\mathcal X_c$ for cells with exactly those counts. A full de novo draw has law
\[
  Q(dc,dx)=\pi(dc)\,Q_{\theta,c}(dx),\qquad
  Q(E)=\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}\pi(c)\int_{\mathcal X_c}{\bf1}_{E}(c,x)\,Q_{\theta,c}(dx).
\]
Formula novelty, element-count distance and chemical validity depend on $\pi$. Coordinate validity, contact distances and density given the same sampled composition probe geometry. Marginal density, structural coverage, formation-energy distributions and relaxed yield generally depend on both factors. An unseen composition with $\pi(c)>0$ has a well-defined generated conditional law, although the training archive supplies no empirical $\widehat P_c$ there. Conditional claims under the empirical training law do not constrain this extrapolation.
