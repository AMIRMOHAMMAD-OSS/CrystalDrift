# One-step drifting for periodic inorganic structures

**Literature checked 20 November 2026.** A defensible question is whether a composition-conditioned generator trained by a crystal-level attraction–repulsion field can predict both a cell and its atomic positions in one network evaluation, and how much accuracy or diversity it loses at that budget. Existing work establishes one-step crystal prediction, symmetry-aware drifting, and manifold kernel-gradient drifting. Searches for “crystal drifting”, “drifting loss” with inorganic crystal structure prediction, “CrystalDrift”, “DriftCSP”, and crystal variants of SymDrift and kernel-gradient drifting located no primary paper running this particular combination. That search supports an application-level research question, without establishing priority. The older [project reading guide](../crystal/reading-guide.md) studies a separate task involving discrete symmetry-site assignments.

## A practical conditional field

Use lattice basis vectors as columns of \(A\), fractional coordinates \(f_i\in\mathbb R^3/\mathbb Z^3\), and prescribed species \(s_i\). A generated sample comprises \(G=A^\top A\) and all \(f_i\). A Cholesky factor with positive diagonal, or a symmetric matrix exponential, yields positive-definite \(G\). Fix a documented primitive/reduced-cell convention if the implementation relies on it. Generator input includes independent atom noise, global noise and the species multiset. Independent atom noise permits same-species atoms to occupy distinct sites. The target cell or target geometry would introduce extra conditioning information if supplied at inference.

One option uses a joint descriptor \(y=\phi_c(G,f)\) that contains cell size/shape and geometry of the decorated motif. For a differentiable, positive affinity \(k(y,z)\), fixed condition \(c\), and frozen reference banks, a kernel-gradient drift in feature space is

\[
v(y)=\nabla_y\log\left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_a k(y,y_a^{\rm real})\right]-\nabla_y\log\left[\frac{1}{r}\sum_b k(y,y_b^{\rm gen})\right].
\]

Estimate the second expectation with fresh independent negatives or exclude the query itself. A detached target \(y+\eta v(y)\) in a squared feature loss gives an update proportional to \(J_\theta(\phi_c\circ g_\theta)^\top v(y)\). This is a gradient in feature space pulled back through the generator. An intrinsic physical gradient in fractional-position/cell space requires a metric there; an unweighted Euclidean gradient in fractional coordinates depends on the chosen basis. The usual Gaussian score-ratio identity follows with separately normalized positive and negative kernel weights. Deng-style two-axis affinity normalization or RMS rescaling gives a related empirical field and needs its own derivation before being identified with this exact log-ratio estimator. [Kernel-gradient drift, §3.2–§4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10727v2)

Exact formula conditioning needs enough independent generated samples per formula for repulsion. When a training formula has one empirical reference, its empirical conditional distribution is a point mass. A claim about recovering its physical polymorph frequencies cannot come from that training set. Pooling positives or negatives across different formulas changes the target field. Document the chemical similarity or stoichiometry rule and evaluate its effect on held-out formulas. Positive cell volume and exact elemental counts are construction checks. Geometry match, contact violations, independent energy after relaxation, and independent diversity measurements answer different scientific questions.

## Which geometric invariances hold

- Translating every \(f_i\) by the same vector and permuting atoms within species preserve the crystal. The descriptor, loss and matched samples can each be checked under these operations. Comparing raw fractional arrays requires atom and origin alignment. Taking a separate optimal alignment for every pair of structures can introduce nondifferentiability when alignments tie.
- A change of lattice basis \(U\in GL(3,\mathbb Z)\) gives \(A'=AU\), \(G'=U^\top G U\), \(f'_i=U^{-1}f_i\). Rotational invariance of a Gram representation alone leaves this ambiguity. Unlike a compact origin torus or a finite species-permutation group, the full unimodular group has no uniform probability distribution over all its elements. A reduced-cell convention is a chart choice with ties and boundaries. Supercells additionally change the number of sites and require explicit primitive reduction or features proven to respect reindexing by cell multiplicity.
- A species-resolved Fourier amplitude \(F_s(h)=\sum_{i:s_i=s}e^{2\pi i h^\top f_i}\) acquires a common phase under translation at fixed reciprocal index \(h\). The products \(F_s(h)\overline{F_t(h)}\) cancel that phase and are permutation-invariant. For an integer basis change, indices map to \(h'=U^\top h\). A fixed coordinate cube of indices is generally not closed under this action. Summing over all reciprocal vectors within a physical cutoff based on \(h^\top G^{-1}h\) with a smooth taper can make a shell descriptor basis-invariant; the implementation must enumerate the complete physical shell. Finite shell averages discard further directional information. Translation-invariant bispectra use \(F_s(h)F_t(k)\overline{F_u(h+k)}\). A finite selection can improve discrimination without guaranteeing it.
- Minimum-image distance uses \(\min_{n\in\mathbb Z^3}(\Delta f+n)^\top G(\Delta f+n)\). Componentwise rounding of \(\Delta f\) can fail for skew cells. With unit basis vectors at 60° and \(\Delta f=(0.49,0.49)\), componentwise rounding gives a vector of length approximately 0.849; the image \((-0.51,0.49)\) has length approximately 0.500. Finite image stencils need a range condition tied to the cell. A Gaussian image-sum kernel on a flat torus accounts for periodicity through a convergent sum over all integer images. A truncated sum is an approximation whose invariance and gradient error need checking. [Riemannian kernels and intrinsic updates](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10727v2#S4)

## Two concrete negative controls

**Homometry.** On the cyclic unit cell \(\mathbb Z/12\), compare the single-species motifs \(A=\{0,1,4,6\}\) and \(B=\{0,1,3,7\}\). Their ordered modular difference histograms are identical: four at displacement zero, two at six and one at each other displacement. Their powers \(|F(h)|^2\) therefore agree for every integer harmonic. Their circular gap multisets differ, \(\{1,2,3,6\}\) versus \(\{1,2,4,5\}\), which rules out both translation and reflection equivalence. A joint cell-plus-power descriptor still maps this pair to the same value when the cell is shared. [Background on homometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.0094)

**Coincident sites.** Let \(L(f_1,\ldots,f_n)\) be a differentiable scalar training potential invariant under common translation and all permutations for a single species. At \(f_1=\cdots=f_n\), permutation invariance forces all coordinate gradients to be equal. Translation invariance forces their sum to be zero. Each gradient therefore vanishes at this configuration, even if the same objective continues moving the cell. This calculation concerns the coordinate component and applies directly to scalar loss functions built from smooth invariant descriptors and detached feature-space targets. It does not establish that ordinary training will collapse. A near-coincidence gradient or minimum-distance diagnostic can measure whether the obstruction is relevant to the fitted model.
